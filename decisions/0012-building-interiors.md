## What's being decided

Walking into buildings would be fun, but it needs:
- photos and floor-plan knowledge
- a lot more art (furniture, counters, stairs)
- care about what we publish: residence hall and office layouts are a security and privacy concern

**Agreed for now (2026-10-09):** the game stays **outdoors only**. Facing a building's door and pressing Z opens a **building card**: the building's name, what's inside (for example "Health Center · Counseling Center · Fordham Store"), and a link to the official page. This file records the options so we can pick it up later.

## Options (for when we revisit)

### A. "Lobby only" for 3–5 key resource buildings
- Buildings would be, for example, the Walsh Library, McShane Campus Center and O'Hare (health and counseling).
- Each is one small room that's *representative*, not accurate: a front desk NPC, a sign or two, an exit.
- **Pros:**
  - Fun for players, at modest cost.
  - Teaches "what happens when you walk in" (for example, what to say at the Health Center desk).
  - Needs no floor plans.
- **Cons:**
  - Some art work: about 10 indoor tiles.
  - Someone might mistake a made-up interior for the real layout.

### B. Accurate interiors from floor plans and photos
- **Pros:** The most useful for wayfinding.
- **Cons:**
  - Lots of research and art.
  - Needs university permission.
  - Security concerns for some buildings.

### C. Never: keep building cards only
- **Pros:** Zero extra work, and nothing to keep accurate.
- **Cons:** Less sense of discovery.

## My recommendation (for later)

**Option A, but only if playtesters ask for it.** Never do residence hall interiors. Building cards cover the onboarding goal ("what's in this building?"). If we do add interiors, the engine already supports them: an interior is just another small map.
