# Part 1: Where you get the bike

### A. A bike rack outside Walsh Library, by the walkway from the gate (recommended, built)

- It's two tiles, just east of the library's signpost, a short walk from where you come in.
- Face it and press **Z**:
  - with no bike, you borrow one and get straight on, and a short message explains X;
  - with a bike, you park it back.

### B. Get a bike from the Orientation Leader at the gate

- **Cons:** The bike rack is something students will actually see on campus.

# Part 2: Riding

### A. X hops on and off anywhere; riding is twice as fast (recommended, built)

- **Speed:** 4 px a frame instead of 2, so each step takes 4 frames instead of 8 and you still stop exactly on a tile.
- **Where:** you can ride anywhere you can walk, including roads, walkways and lawns.
- **Feedback:** hopping on or off shows a small "ON THE BIKE" or "WALKING" note at the bottom.
- **Persistence:** the bike stays yours until you put it back in a rack.
- **Controls card:** now reads "X: Back, skip, bike on/off".

### B. Ride only on roads and walkways

- **Pros:** More realistic.
- **Cons:** Players would get stuck mid-lawn and have to work out why.

## What was built

- **Bike logic:** `game/systems/bike.py`, pure and tested in `tests/test_bike.py`.
- **Overworld:** `game/scenes/overworld.py` handles the rack, X, the speed, the sprite and the note.
- **New tile:** `bike_rack` (two maroon bikes in a gray rack).
- **New sprite row:** the player riding (light-blue bike, turning pedals). Both are in `game/gfx/placeholder_art.py`.
- **Config:** `config.BIKE_SPEED = 4`.
- **Map:** the rack is at (20, 71) and (21, 71).
- **Tree placement:** it now counts signs, NPCs and racks as solid. A tree could otherwise have closed off the lawn by the library.
- **Screenshots:** `tools/capture.py` adds `17ba_bike_rack`, `17bb_riding` (riding to Larkin) and `17bc_hop_off`.
