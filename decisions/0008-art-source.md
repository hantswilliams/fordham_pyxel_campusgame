## What's being decided

Art is usually the slowest part of a game like this. Who makes the real tiles (grass, paths, gothic stone, windows, doors, trees) and character sprites?

## Options

### A. Keep placeholders through M3, then original art made in `pyxel edit` (recommended)
- The art would be drawn by you, a student artist, or me improving the code-drawn art.
- **Pros:**
  - Fully original, with no licensing questions.
  - Can capture Fordham's actual look (collegiate gothic stone, maroon banners).
  - Pyxel's 16-color, 8×8 format keeps the job small: about 30 tiles and 5–6 characters.
- **Cons:** Someone has to draw it. Expect a few days of focused work.

### B. Adapt free (CC0) tilesets from OpenGameArt or itch.io
- **Pros:** Looks decent quickly.
- **Cons:**
  - Generic fantasy and town styles don't look like Fordham.
  - Every pack needs remapping to Pyxel's 16-color palette.
  - Every license must be checked.

### C. Commission a pixel artist
- **Pros:** The best quality.
- **Cons:** Costs money, and needs a brief and a review cycle.

## My recommendation

**Option A.** Gameplay first, art second: walking, talking, interiors and quests should feel right before anyone spends time on art. Then a small original tileset will look much more like Fordham than a generic pack. If the project gets university backing, C becomes a good upgrade for the public launch.
