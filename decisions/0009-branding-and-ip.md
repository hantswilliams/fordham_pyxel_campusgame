## What's being decided

Two IP questions:
1. **Fordham:** the name, logo, maroon and the Ram are university trademarks.
2. **Pokémon:** Nintendo, Game Freak and The Pokémon Company actively protect their characters, names, sprites and music.

## Options

### A. Fordham name and colors now; Marketing sign-off before public launch; Pokémon-inspired but no Pokémon assets (recommended, already built)
- Use "Fordham," real building names and maroon in development and internal testing.
- Before any public release, get approval from Fordham's marketing and communications office, along with official color values.
- Don't use the Fordham logo or Ram mark until approved.
- Borrow Pokémon *mechanics* only (grid movement, text boxes, a collection index, badges). Use no Pokémon names, creatures, sprites, sounds or music, and no "Poké-" words.
- **Pros:**
  - Feels like Fordham from day one.
  - Stays clear of Pokémon legal risk.
  - Gets university buy-in before launch, which is also how this becomes official orientation material.
- **Cons:** Launch depends on a university approval step.

### B. Unbranded "University" until approval
- **Pros:** Zero risk at any stage.
- **Cons:**
  - Feels generic, so it's harder to judge whether the game works for Fordham students.
  - Means redoing text later.

### C. Use the Fordham logo and Ram mascot freely now
- **Pros:** The most recognizable.
- **Cons:** Risks a takedown request, and starts the relationship with the university on the wrong foot.

## My recommendation

**Option A.** Real names and colors make playtesting meaningful. Asking Marketing before launch is something you'd want anyway, because an official link from orientation is the best way to reach students. The Pokémon line is simple: copy how it *plays*, never what it *looks or sounds* like.
