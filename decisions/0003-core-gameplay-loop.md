## What's being decided

"Pokémon-like" can mean many things. What keeps a student playing for 15–25 minutes, and what do they remember afterwards?

## Options

### A. Explore + collect + checklist + trivia, no combat (recommended)
- **Campus Guide:** a Pokédex-style index that fills in as you discover buildings and offices. Each entry has a short description and a link to the real page.
- **First Week Checklist:** the main quest. Get your ID, find the library, eat at the student center, find wellness and career resources, and so on.
- **Trivia challenges:** RAs and orientation leaders challenge you to 3 questions, Pokémon-trainer style. Answering earns badges (Academics, Wellness, Community, Career, Dining).
- **Pros:**
  - Every mechanic teaches something real.
  - The checklist gives a clear finish line.
  - Badges and the collection give the Pokémon "gotta catch 'em all" pull.
- **Cons:** Trivia needs well-written questions, which is content work for staff.

### B. Pure exploration (walk around, read signs, talk to people)
- **Pros:** Simplest, with the least content.
- **Cons:**
  - No goals, so most players wander for 2 minutes and quit.
  - Hard to measure whether anyone learned anything.

### C. Pokémon-style turn-based "quiz battles" (HP bars, attack = answer a question)
- **Pros:** The most Pokémon-like and fun to watch.
- **Cons:**
  - A lot more UI and animation work.
  - Combat framing sits oddly with "go to the counseling center."
  - Risks feeling like a Pokémon clone (see D-0009).

## My recommendation

**Option A.** It keeps the parts of Pokémon that make people keep playing (collecting, badges, trainers who stop you to challenge you) and drops combat, which costs a lot to build and doesn't fit an onboarding game. If testers love the trivia, we can dress it up later with a battle-style screen. That's a presentation change, not a redesign.
